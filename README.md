# I REMEMBER BEING A BUTTERFLY

### a halfworld

An 8-minute film in which every image is code. No photographs, no footage, no samples, no stock
audio. Forty scenes, twelve cards, five faces, a score and nineteen foley events, all generated by
the modules in this repository — which means the film is not an artefact stored here, it is an
*output* of here, and it rebuilds from source.

```bash
node harness/serve.mjs        # → http://127.0.0.1:8181/periodic.html
```

That page is the front door: a **periodic table of the world's eighty-one elements**, each one
rendered live by the module that defines it, and a chat that stages them.

---

## THE STORY

A butterfly in a Y-maze is offered sugar in both arms. One arm also carries lavender. It takes
neither. It flies at the ceiling and strikes it, over and over, in a pattern, until the pattern is
a shape.

Two researchers disagree about whether that is data. An old woman arrives with a film can and a
grocery bag, says *"you have her shoulders"*, and turns out to have run the same experiment
seventy years earlier. The building she takes them to is full of dated, empty chrysalides going
back to 1951, and she says she did not build it. She says she **remembered** it.

## WHAT IT STANDS ON, AND WHERE IT DEPARTS

The film has three real referents and one invented act, and the invented act is only legible if you
know the real ones. All of this is in [`atlas/references.json`](atlas/references.json) and stated
plainly in the end roll.

**Joe Nagai**, a ten-year-old, presented work at the International Congress of Entomology in Kyoto
in 2024: swallowtails trained to avoid a scent as caterpillars still avoided it as butterflies.
Untrained animals split 50/50 between the arms; trained ones avoided the scented arm about 70% of
the time. He found **Martha Weiss**'s moth work — a learned aversion surviving pupation, contested
because the received view was that metamorphosis wipes the insect brain — and repeated its shape.

**The substitution is the film's engine and it is a real detail.** The standard protocol uses ethyl
acetate, a killing agent. Nagai judged that caterpillars might be innately sensitive to it, and
replaced it with **lavender oil** because it was pleasanter for animals he considered friends. He
tested the shock levels on his own arm first.

In this film lavender is the trauma cue. **The mercy and the injury are the same molecule.** Nothing
in the film says this out loud and everything in it depends on it.

**莊周夢蝶** — Zhuang Zhou dreams he is a butterfly, wakes, and does not know which he is. The
film's title is that parable **with the doubt taken out**: a flat claim of continuous memory across
a transformation, asserted by something that cannot possibly verify it.

**The departure:** in the experiment there are exactly two outcomes and both are *choices between
two arms*. The animal in this film produces a third that is in neither dataset — it refuses both and
draws on the ceiling. That is not avoidance and it is not indifference. It is a refusal of the
premise, and a refusal is only legible against the choices it declines.

**What is contested:** memory across metamorphosis sits on established ground. Memory across three
generations is a much stronger claim, came from a student project, and is *reported* here rather
than settled. The film does not need it to be true. It needs someone to have wanted it to be.

## THE LAWS

A halfworld is a world with rules strict enough that the rules do the authoring. There are five.

### 1. The dot law

Eight ink levels, ordered halftone, hard black contour, cream paper. **No gradients, no blur, no
alpha.** Everything is drawn in flat tones into an offscreen buffer and a single halftone pass runs
over the whole field at the end, so a face and a wall are quantised by the same lattice.

The constraint is generative rather than decorative. Because there is no alpha, **a dissolve cannot
be a cross-fade** — it has to be a per-dot allegiance swap on an ordered schedule, which reads as one
substance *replacing* another rather than two briefly coexisting. The film is about a substitution.
The constraint and the content agree.

### 2. The unit of authorship is a cycle, not a picture

The text's central images do not exist in any single frame:

> *"The mark existed only when the wings were closed together."*
> *"The line gathered over itself, loop after loop, until a figure appeared."*
> *"a synchronized line passing across the wall from left to right"*

A still cannot hold a difference-between-states, an accumulation, or a wavefront. So every scene is
a **pure function of normalised time** `u ∈ [0,1)` and nothing else — no wall clock, no accumulated
state — which makes frame *n* reproducible without frame *n−1* ever having existed.

There are **ten motions**, taken from the text rather than imposed on it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **CYCLE** | a body that comes round again — *never closed*, because a closed cycle is apparatus, not an animal |
| **TRACE** | a line gathering over itself |
| **SWEEP** | a wavefront crossing a field |
| **TRANSFER** | material moving between surfaces; it *brightens* at the midpoint, nothing dims |
| **ADVANCE** | discrete steps with a dwell |
| **DISSOLVE** | the ordered per-dot swap |
| **HOLD** | not a freeze — a breath under 1% of frame, deniable |
| **BREAK** | the one permitted discontinuity, declared in the module that uses it |
| **SPEAK** | added for the close-ups. A sentence runs once and stops |
| **LISTEN** | a face responding to a voice it is not making |

### 3. The blocking law

Bodies are staged by **name**, not by coordinate. Each of the five places is a *plan* — a coordinate
space with named stations (`iona_stand`, `stair_foot`, `chrysalis_both_hands` — 56 in the facility
alone) — and a scene asks for a station. An unknown name throws. You cannot put someone somewhere
the world does not have.

Depth is one law shared by every plan: `y = 0.50 + 0.46·d^1.08`.

### 4. Measure the ink

Nothing is cropped to a bounding box, ever. Assets are measured by where their ink actually falls,
which is why a figure can be given a box and be trusted to fill it.

### 5. A ledger with no failures is a falsified ledger

Every build stage writes what it *saw* and what it *changed*. The docs in this repo record the
mistakes as prominently as the results, because in four passes of work the pattern held every single
time: **every serious defect produced a plausible picture, and every one was caught by looking at an
image rather than by a test.**

## THE PERIODIC TABLE

`node harness/serve.mjs` → **[periodic.html](periodic.html)**

The world is a small number of **primitives** that combine and fifty-seven **compounds** that are
combinations of them. That is not a metaphor applied afterwards — it is how the repo is built. A
scene imports two or three assets, declares one of ten motions, names one of five plans, and lists
its sound cues. Nothing else.

So BF-17 is not "a scene". It is:

```
Mi·St·Ml·Ki·Io·Ho·Rm·T1·Co·Se
```

— a mill, a stage, the mill's set module, the creature kit, Iona, a HOLD, the mill's room tone, the
arrival theme, a coat and a footstep. Written that way you can see that BF-18, BF-19, BF-20, BF-22
and BF-25 are the same columns with one substitution, which is a fact about the film that watching
it will not tell you.

**Nothing in the table is hand-typed.** `harness/build-atlas-index.mjs` reads the elements out of the
filesystem and the formulas out of each scene's own imports and exports. Change a scene's imports and
the table changes. An atlas that has to be maintained by hand is wrong within a week.

### each group shows itself the way it deserves

There are no thumbnails on that page. Clicking Iona **imports `assets/character/iona.mjs`**, calls
her `draw()` into an offscreen canvas, and runs the same halftone pass the film runs.

- **MOTION** — plotted. The curve *is* the definition, sampled from the real function, with a
  playhead. A motion is a function, so it is drawn as a function.
- **CAST / ANIMAL** — their own `draw()`, with their **state machine** and **motion channels** as
  buttons. Iona has seven states and three motions; click `expression_early` and watch the thing the
  text asks for — *"expressions arriving before the features could contain them"*.
- **PLACE** — a plan is a coordinate space, so it is drawn as a **map**: every station projected
  through the same depth law the film stages bodies with.
- **SET** — its exported functions, listed. Most set modules are libraries with different
  signatures and there is nothing honest to auto-render, so the page says so rather than inventing a
  call and printing a broken picture.
- **SOUND / FOLEY / SCORE** — the waveform, and it plays.

### staging by chat

```
mara and niko in the laboratory, cycle
iona close
the swallowtail in the maze
```

Deliberately **a parser, not a model call.** The vocabulary of this world is eighty-one named
things; a parser over eighty-one names is not a degraded understanding, it is the right tool — it
works offline with no key, and it *cannot hallucinate an element that does not exist*, which a model
asked to "stage a scene" absolutely will.

## THE FILM

| | |
|---|---|
| `film/BUTTERFLY.mp4` | the wide cut — every scene as its own loop |
| `film/BUTTERFLY-CLOSE.mp4` | the speaking cut — 8.09 min, stereo |

Both are the same length and share one sound master, sample for sample.

### the speaking cut

Twelve people speak for 3m11s and in the wide cut **no visible mouth ever opens.** The close-up
system fixes that, and its one idea is:

> **The waveform decides *if* the mouth is open. The text decides *what shape* it is in.**

Every lip-sync failure is one of those doing the other's job. Text alone flaps through silence;
envelope alone is a hinge that never closes on an `/m/`, because during a bilabial the voice
continues and only the lips stop.

The head is built around one anatomical fact: **the upper lip belongs to the skull, the lower lip
rides the jaw.** `jaw` is not a mouth parameter here, it is a *bone* — the chin travels, the face
lengthens, and the aperture is a consequence of rotating it rather than a drawing of it.

### and then only thirteen of them

The first speaking cut gave a close-up to all ninety speaker runs, because that is a rule and rules
are easy to apply ninety times. 45% of the picture became a talking head and it got there by cutting
away from the film's best images. The law it should have used was already in the repo:

> **A close-up may only interrupt a motion that comes round again.**

CYCLE repeats and HOLD persists — leave one and come back and it is still doing what it was doing.
TRACE, SWEEP, DISSOLVE, ADVANCE, TRANSFER and BREAK happen once and *accumulate*; leaving one means
the audience misses the only time it happens. Sixteen scenes were never candidates.

The law says *permitted*, not *deserved*, so [`harness/direct.mjs`](harness/direct.mjs) carries
fifteen **authored turns**, each with the line it is for and the argument for it. Thirteen survive
the budget. **7.4% of the picture is a face.** Two are reaction shots — the camera on the person
*receiving* the line, which is the shot the film most needed and had none of.

### sound

The score's one idea: **it and the room tone are the same material at different densities.** Every
room already hums — `room_lab` is mains at 60Hz — so the score is rooted on **60Hz, the frequency the
laboratory hums at**, and is what happens when that hum resolves into pitch. There is no moment where
music starts, which is also why a cut cannot break it.

The material is the film's own. The **pulse** is the butterfly's tapping at half rate. The **theme**
is the chrysalis wall's dates, 1951→2024, read left to right as an ascending line. The
**substitution cue** obeys the picture's dissolve law — the two chords exchange *harmonic by
harmonic* rather than cross-fading. The film opens in **Aeolian** and ends in **Dorian**: one note
different, which is what a chrysalis is. Thirty-eight scenes carry no music at all.

The mix places every character in a fixed seat — **Mara left, Niko right, Iona centre** — because the
ear locates a speaker before it identifies a timbre. And in-room voices go through the **location's
early reflections** while voice-overs get none, which is the film's only audible distinction between
a voice and a present body. In a film about testimony that cannot be checked, that distinction is the
subject.

## LAYOUT

```
engine/          halfworld-engine (dot law) · motion (8) · speech (viseme + listen)
                 figure-hero · blocking · guise · liveness
assets/          character/ (5 + the close-up puppet) · creature/ · set/
scenes/          BF-01..BF-40 · C00..C05 cards · P00..P04 place slates · CREDITS
  _plans/        the five coordinate spaces
  _close/        95 generated close-ups, one per thing somebody says
audio/           script · scenes/*.wav · sound/ (beds) · foley/ (events) · score/
atlas/           source.json · references.json · periodic.json
harness/         every build stage, each with its own instrument
film/            the cuts, and the JSON that describes them
```

### rebuilding

```bash
node harness/measure-lines.mjs        # line boundaries, measured off the waveform
node harness/build-closeups.mjs       # 95 close-up modules
node harness/direct.mjs               # which 13 earn a face
node harness/render-motion.mjs --all  # every scene → webm
node harness/build-sound.mjs          # room beds
node harness/build-foley.mjs          # 19 events
node harness/build-score.mjs          # 9 cues
node harness/build-master.mjs         # the stereo mix
node harness/build-film-close.mjs     # the speaking cut
node harness/build-atlas-index.mjs    # the periodic table
```

### the instruments

Every stage carries a way of being caught out, and they have all earned it:

| | |
|---|---|
| `measure-lines` | how far each boundary moved from the word-count guess. **17 of 72 moved by over half a second** — those are cuts that would have landed on the wrong face |
| `render-motion` | loop closure: last frame vs frame 0. `--endpoint-bug` reruns with the wrong divisor to prove the check detects the fault it exists to detect |
| `measure-frames` | ink coverage and value spread per scene, weighted by **actual on-screen time in the cut** |
| `gate` | seven gates that exit non-zero and each name which stage fixes them |

## THE MISTAKES

Kept because they are the most useful thing here.

**The eyes were half a head too high.** The first close-up puppet came back long-jawed and I blamed
the jaw. People place eyes too high because the cranium above them is under hair and the brain
discounts what it cannot see. Everything below them had lengthened to compensate.

**The anvil.** A scalloped shape on the cheek appeared on Iona, The Girl and Mara and *never* on
Niko. I read it as a hairstyle bug and rewrote the hair code **twice**. It was `jawW` being an
absolute multiplier: at 0.70 the gonion sat 0.43R inside the cheekbone across 0.32R of drop and the
curve overshot. The pattern that should have told me — *only the narrow-jawed faces* — is exactly
what I misread.

**The instrument said a good shot was blank.** `measure-frames` reported BF-37 at 0.0% ink. BF-37 is
one of the best shots in the film; the sample had landed inside an authored **whiteout**. Scenes are
now decoded whole and reported at their *median* frame.

**Zooming does not create ink.** Told that frames were empty, I pushed the lens in on the three worst
and made two of them worse. There was nothing behind the subject to get closer to — BF-18's own
comment said so: *"two broken lines a long way off, and paper."* The mill has surfaces. It was using
none of them.

**A generator deleted another generator's work, twice.** `build-cards` wrote `{cards: ORDER}` and
silently dropped every entry it did not own. The end roll fell out of the film twice, the second time
unnoticed until the cut came back 67 seconds short.

**Say less.** The framing cards lost their body copy to the dot floor. I halved the copy — and it
still dissolved, because the test was never size, it was **case**: a lowercase serif is ascenders,
thin joins and tiny counters, all at or under the dot pitch at any size this frame holds. Then the
first credit roll came in at *two minutes on a seven-minute film.* A constraint asks you to say
less, and three separate times I answered by saying the same amount more carefully.
