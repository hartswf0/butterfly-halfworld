Three programs, three answers to where time comes from. The archive radio finds time — its verbs are NEXT, RANDOM, LOOP, and the cadence is already inside the recording. The WYGWYL suite shares time — one audio element is the clock and every medium is a lookup on it, frame = (t − start) × 8. Sketchsong constructs time — FROM → THROUGH → TO, with the translation theory written where you can argue with it.

The difference that makes a difference is where their arrows point. In the suite player every medium is a child of t and none is a parent of any other: poem, film, frame and drone are siblings joined on a key, and nothing in that program can make the drone respond to the poem. A join is not a relation. In Sketchsong the arrow between media exists and is editable — the real advance — but it is compiled by a language model, runs one way per generation, and is not live. What is missing from all three is an arrow that is live, reversible and measurable.

So: a correspondence must be falsifiable. Every arrow here runs twice each frame, once with it and once with it muted, and the difference it made to its target is printed beside it. An arrow that changes nothing says DEAD. And the fourteen prosodic engines were already relation graphs — sweep, gate, ground, attack are four arrows whose source is hard-wired to the clock. With no arrows at all, this page is exactly the sketch radio.

The poet is here as timing and text only — 111 beats lifted from the suite's twin EDLs. Not as audio. So POET hears nothing and affects everything by construction rather than by good intentions, and the line being spoken is inked into the same field the head crosses: the poem is the picture, and the picture is the sound.  ·  space plays.
