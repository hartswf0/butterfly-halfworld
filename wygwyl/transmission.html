THE TRANSMISSION

the readings as broadcast radio — each poem a station on the dial. The voice is first class: the whole reading, untouched, at its own pacing. Under it, tape loops of three different lengths disintegrate as they repeat — darker, dropping out, fluttering into fractured echoes — her own words drift through the silences two octaves down, and the dial itself wanders once, in her longest breath. Every render is rewhispered: the engine transcribes its own output, and if the mix ate her words, the pass fails and it clears the air. What survived is logged below, station by station.
