WYGWYL · 01 OUT OF LIFE · A TEST SUITE, NOT A FILM How to hide AI crimes with halfworld.

The footage is synthetic and it looks synthetic: smooth gradients, an uncanny face, temporal shimmer, light too clean to be real. The dot law removes every one of those by construction — eight ink levels kill the gradient, a hard lattice kills the shimmer, and there is no face left at 192×144 to be uncanny. So halftoning this footage is not a filter laid over it; it is a way of making it true. Every cell below is one way of doing that, run live against the same eighty-eight seconds of film — a poet, a dim graffitied room, a window on a foggy street, a fall through sparks. Look for the ones where you can no longer tell what was filmed and what was drawn.

"words become worlds, and halfworld becomes a full world" — the other half of the same idea. A few cells here go looking for the real footage inside the drawn room, and the drawn room inside the real one — not two layers, one substance.

HOW TO READ THIS PAGE

Every cell is live: the halftone, the crossfades and the parameter sweeps all run in real time in your browser, off the same law every WYGWYL film obeys — eight ink levels, one ordered halftone pass, no alpha, no gradient. What is not live everywhere is the camera: this sandbox's Chromium ships without H.264 decode, so the footage cells are driven by pre-extracted stills standing in for <video> frames (see frames/ and NOTES.md). The code path is identical either way — ingest.mjs's own videoWidth||naturalWidth fallback is what makes a still and a video frame indistinguishable to everything downstream — and this page tries the real file first; open it in a browser with the codec and the footage cells upgrade to true video with no code change.

Two cells at the top are not treatments — a plain source frame and a plain drawn frame, side by side, so the other twenty-eight have something to be measured against.
