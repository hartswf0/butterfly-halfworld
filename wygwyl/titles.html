WHERE YOU GO WHEN YOU LEAVE · TITLE SEQUENCE, FOUR WAYS A title card in the manner of John Whitney.

Fourteen films need one thing in front of them, and Whitney's own rule for a title is that it is never typed on — it is what a field of moving elements looks like at the instant their motion happens to agree with itself. Every option below is a single differential law, run for its own thirty seconds, with the words of the suite's own title read directly out of the law's resonant moments rather than drawn over the top of it.

All four are the real films, running live below, not a thumbnail of one — the same drawPreview the fourteen-film index uses. Open any card for its own page, transport and sound.

THE FOUR DEVICES

A · The Rosette. One dense field on differential phase — point k turns k times the base rate about its own anchor. Nothing else moves it; the loops, flowers and caustics are what nineteen hundred clocks running at nineteen hundred different speeds look like from above, and the title is a cell lifted from that same field at the moment it resonates.

B · The Arabesque. Six rings, each turning at an integer multiple of one rate and alternating direction, breathing in and out of phase with each other. The two lines of the title are built by two different generations of rings collapsing inward in turn, while the rest of the arabesque keeps running behind them.

C · The Catalogue. A grid of independent Lissajous cells, each one's frequency ratio drifting by a constant amount every step until it passes an integer and the figure snaps to a closed loop — a resonance solved backward so it lands on a chosen instant. Letter by letter, left to right, a cell's loop is swapped for its own slice of the word and stays.

D · The Aura. B and C fused, and the one that plays at the head of the suite. The rings are the ground and never stop; the Lissajous cells live on them rather than in a grid, and each cell's frequency pair is derived from the ring it rides — so it is still one law read at two distances. What neither of the others had: at each resonance the field also throws up an image from the films behind it — the tambourine, the rose window, the moon, the temple, the eleven-petalled daisy, the burning hourglass, the ride, the candle, the named stars. They arrive faint, hold for a breath, and the rotation takes them back. If you have seen the films they are memories; if you have not they are ornament, and both readings are correct. This one plays the record rather than the synth — the suite's own drone piece is its bed.
