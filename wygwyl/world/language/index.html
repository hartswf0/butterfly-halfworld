Atlantis: a city that keeps its flood, mends itself in ceramic and light, and is skinned with its own footage.

Most film cities are a style. This one is a set of rules that a place could have arrived at. Each rule is readable from the street, each is procedural (the city applies it to all 9,668 buildings) and each comes from the archive or the poems. None is a mood board.

The signature: seven rules

The flood datum

The water of the Fall stood at four metres. Every building that was there carries the same salt-white line at +4.0 m, damp and green below it. It is one level across the whole city, so you can read the disaster in any street, and its absence tells you a house is new.

facade shader · born ≤ New Boroughs and low-lying · world height 3.3–4.45 m

Kintsugi of the Resurrection

Ruins are not replaced, they are repaired: white ceramic plates over the old brick, with their seams drawn in cyan light. A ceramic crown sits on top. It is the same ceramic as the white temple's vault and the evolved towers, so you can tell the new city by its material.

kind 2 (reuse) · voronoi plates in metres · seams emissive, brighter at night

Every house wears its additions

Lighter, later boxes sit set back on old roofs, in wood or stucco, and arrived eras after the house. Height tells you age; colour tells you which layer.

3,319 additions · born per era · own material

Stairs are streets

Circulation happens outside, where you can see it: marble stoops, iron back stairs, fire escapes, the stepped avenue. He looks for a way out "through the hallways, within the drawers and fire escapes", and the city keeps those exits visible.

streamed detail · stoops on rowhouses · back stairs on 22% of the tall old houses

The tide keeps the time

The sea gates rise and fall with a real 12.42-hour tide, and the city's light comes as a pulse. Touch a landmark and a ring of cyan runs out across every facade: the city resonates.

semidiurnal tide · gate height from tide · pulse ring in the facade shader

Skinned with its own footage

Brick, peeling paint, stucco, asphalt, marble steps, moss and graffiti are cut from frames in the archive (generated stills first) and made seamless. Each district's colours come from its own evidence images.

grit.py + library.py · 32 surfaces × 2 variants · k-means palettes per district

Inverse monuments

The sci-fi structures stand where the shots ask for them. Spires, halos, arcologies and sea walls are placed by evolution where they answer the most pictures, so every monument has witnesses.

structures.py · 33 structures answer 250 of 416 calls · see them

The small things: the Line and the Seam

A city is believed through its small things. Every object in Atlantis, from a street lamp to a manhole cover, carries the same two marks. The Line is a salt band at 4/10 of the object's height, the flood remembered at every scale; on the line posts it sits at exactly +4.00 m above the sea. The Seam is a cyan crack drawn in light, where something broke and was mended. The materials have fixed jobs: ceramic for what you touch, verdigris iron for what holds up, cyan only for light and repair, salt only for memory. Rings mark every threshold: the gates, the halo, the lamp heads, the tide board, the emblem.

The emblem

A ring (the gates, the tide) cut by the Line at 4/10 of its height and crossed by a seam from top to bottom. It is stamped on the manhole covers, set on the tide boards and the poem plaques, and flown on First Tide.

The law of small things

Every object carries the Line at 4/10 of its height. A line post carries it at +4.00 m above the sea, so you can read the walls against it.

Cyan is only ever light or repair. A seam means something here broke and was mended.

Ceramic where hands go, verdigris where weight goes, iron where nobody looks.

A threshold is a ring: lamp heads, gates, the halo, the board's crest, the emblem.

Benches face the water wherever there is water.

Information is mono, names are serif, the poems are italic, and none of them is ever shouted in capitals except a measure.

Nothing hides the flood line: no awning, sign or planter is hung across +4 m.

Districts: colours from their own pictures, surfaces from the archive

Found architecture: buildings that exist only in our footage

I searched every frame that isn't from the real-world archive (our generated stills, the butterfly Markov clips, halfworld frames and renders) for architecture, and ranked each frame by how unlike the 45,000 real archive frames it is. I clustered the best into species. For each species, the most architectural and most unique member is segmented and cut out with its own outline. In the city its outline becomes the extrusion and the frame becomes its face, on a lot in a district that suits what it is. An eye-review gate keeps only real buildings: 14 of 30 cut-outs passed.

The material library: cut from the archive

For each surface, the crop that CLIP reads most as that surface is cut from the top archive hits, scaled to 256 px and blended seamless (four-way, each copy weighted to zero at its own seam). The city samples these in metres, mip-safe across tile edges, and fades them out past 700 m so the distance stays calm.

The grit inventory: what the archive holds, and what it lacks

Collect next: the gaps

The archive is rich in atmosphere: puddles, bus stops at night, streetlights, floods, doorways. It is poor in the everyday material of a lived-in street. These are the elements with the fewest strong frames, each with a generation prompt written in the city's own language, so new footage arrives already belonging to Atlantis.
