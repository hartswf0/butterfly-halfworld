Next passes

Nine pieces of work, each traced to the tools it starts from.

Evolve the interiors

Every foundry shot assumes the character is outside a building. The city was evolved; the rooms never were. Run the same evolutionary pass per building: genome = room programme, light source, threshold, furniture, who is there; fitness = the poem's lines and the archive's interior frames.

FromShots, Hero buildings, Archive

Inverse tomography

Inverse topology reads the land back from the shots. Turn it inward: from the shots that look into or out of a structure (windows, doorways, light spill), reconstruct the section of the building — what must be inside for that frame to exist. The output is a needs-list per interior.

FromInverse topology, Point clouds

Dress the sets

The landscape per shot is bare: no people, cars, neighbouring buildings, interiors, aura or lights. Add a dressing layer to every set, and mock scenes up faster by compositing cutouts over the clips we already have instead of regenerating.

FromSets, Fourteen halfworlds (cutout source), The Weave (registration)

One believable city, built from the foundry

Five 3D cities, none convincing: oversized buildings, heavy shaders, grain, bad ground. Stop patching them separately. Take the Island as the base, generate its fabric from the foundry and inverse work, and port the best feature of each other map into it — movie-map routes, imageable-city structure, mood lighting.

FromIsland, Movie map, Imageable city, City across time

Inspect every building

Hero buildings have lights and shaders that are sometimes off, and no aura. Give each building an inspection sheet — scale, material, light, aura, matching archive references, interior status — and fail the ones that don't pass before they enter the city.

FromHero buildings, Design language

Sound, time, swappability

The world is silent, has no clock, and is hard to re-skin. Give each district a sound bed and a day cycle driven by one shared clock, with sound and look loaded from data so a world can be swapped. Source ambiences from Freesound (API, Creative Commons — keep the attribution list). Start from the duets; avoid the deep echoed voice and the busyness of the radio and poem worlds.

FromDuets, Observatory, Freesound

Invisible Cities as the frame

Calvino's fifty-five cities are one city, Venice, redescribed. Fourteen poems redescribing one Atlantis is the same device. The foundry is Polo reporting to the Khan; the gazetteer is the Khan's atlas; Calvino's eleven rubrics (memory, desire, signs, thin, trading, eyes, names, the dead, sky, continuous, hidden) are a ready tagging scheme for districts and shots. Inverse topology already has its Calvino city: Eusapia, which keeps an underground copy of itself, and Valdrada, built on its reflection.

FromFoundry, Gazetteer, Scene book

Verne's Atlantis

In Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (part two, chapter nine) Nemo walks Aronnax across the seabed at night to a drowned city lit by an erupting volcano, and writes its name on a rock. Plato gives the ring plan; Verne gives the approach: on foot, underwater, by firelight. A second reading to lay over the Nolli plan.
