The Foundry — worlds, read against the poems

“Our language can be seen as an ancient city: a maze of little streets and squares, of old and new houses, and of houses with additions from various periods; and this surrounded by a multitude of new boroughs with straight regular streets and uniform houses.” — Wittgenstein, Philosophical Investigations §18

Each world here is grown in the poems’ order, not the map-maker’s. First the narrative: three realms (Baltimere, the Center, Atlantis) and the journey’s links — walk, ride, fall, fly, cut, dissolve. Then the raw grid, laid down as a palimpsest of epochs by different algorithms that cut through, give way to, or lie over each other. Then a topography, either fitted to that grid (mountains rise where the city stops, passes open where roads cross) or laid first from the poems’ needs. Then the poems read the world: is the chapel on a shore, is the temple raised on its ruins, can you walk from the night beach up to the meadow and see the Light Gates, does the Magic Ride climb a mountain road into first light, does the café see Atlantis across the water?

No single score is climbed. The map below keeps one world per kind of world; the shelf keeps the strangest ones, including those the poems reject — the step that leads somewhere rarely looks like where it leads.
