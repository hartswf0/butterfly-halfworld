Inverse topology — the shots shape the land

We already have extraordinary shots for these poems: the generated stills of every shot in the bible, the MARKOV_POET clips routed stanza by stanza, the archive footage the Cineosis forage found for each beat, the Halfworld frames. Forward, a world is grown and then photographed. Here it runs the other way: start from the best images for each poem and ask what the land must be for that frame to exist — then sculpt every world toward it.

THE SHOTS
CHOOSECLIP ranks every image against its poem’s place, light and set pieces — and against every other poem, so a strong image in the wrong pool is still found
READa panoptic segmentation into the world’s own classes — sky, water, city, street, forest, meadow, beach, rock — plus horizon and light
FINDsearch each world for the camera that already comes closest: position on the poem’s ground, heading, height
INFERevery missing region becomes a place: its column a bearing, its row below the horizon a distance, its height above it an elevation
SCULPTcarve the water, lay the beach, plant the forest, raise the ridge to its angle, stand the city up to its skyline, open the sky
MANY WORLDSthe same shots pull every world differently — a topology inferred from its photographs

Bigger than this

Clips, not stills. A clip is a camera path and a duration. Read its motion (pan, dolly, rise) and the inverse gives a route through the land, not a point — the Magic Ride’s palm promenade becomes a road the world must lay down.
The reference as a reader. Beside the poems and the builder, a third reader: can this world give back its shots? Evolve against it, and worlds are bred by their photographs.
Cross-poem finds are places. When CLIP pulls the same chapel image for poem 3 and poem 11, the images are saying what the bible says: one site at two times. Shared images become shared ground.
Interiors become sets. References with no horizon — the bedroom, the party, the hall — don’t shape terrain; they shape the island’s walk-in sets: their walls, light and lenses.
Every shot a triple. The found image, the staged set, the rendered world: the film cuts between all three, each matched to the same line.
A map as a sum of shots. Not a map drawn and then filmed, but a map that is whatever land makes all of its shots true at once — and where two shots disagree, a fork: two worlds.
