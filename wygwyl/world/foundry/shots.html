The shots — the poem steers the camera through the world

Each poem’s reading is cut into shots. The line decides the camera: the first line opens wide and high; when he names a thing — the forest, the water, the gates, the temple — the camera looks at it where it actually stands in that world; when he names the sky it looks up; when he turns inward it goes close over his shoulder; and if he is walking or riding on, every shot is taken further along his route to the next poem. The light follows the reading (dusk into night in the tower, night into first light on the Magic Ride). Every frame keeps what is measured in it, so you can search for what you need.

One poem through one world
