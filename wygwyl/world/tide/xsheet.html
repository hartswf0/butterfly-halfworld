The Exposure Sheet — when to say what to the world

An animator does not draw every frame; they draw keys, mark the holds, and let the in-betweens follow. The same sheet works for a world model. This page is generated from his readings and the scene book: one sheet per poem, saying what is sent, when, and what a director should look for afterwards.

SEED = THE PASSAGEOne picture and one paragraph start a session. Sessions stay under 90 seconds; a longer reading reseeds at the scene change nearest the middle, from its last frame.
KEY = THE SCENEOnly a change of place is sent as an event, in 12 words or fewer, at least 4 seconds apart. It goes in the breath before the line, so the place arrives as he names it.
BREAKDOWN = THE LIGHTWhere one place is held more than 22 seconds, one cue changes light and air only, so nothing solid moves.
HOLD = THE SILENCEIn his pauses nothing is sent and the walker stops. Lines that name no place are never sent; his voice carries them.
CHECK = THE DIRECTORAfter each key a director model looks at a frame for the things the shot lab found in that scene's pictures, and sends one short correction if they are missing.

All fourteen once through is 21 minutes of session time, about $127 at the preview rate you quoted. No session has been run; these sheets are a plan.
