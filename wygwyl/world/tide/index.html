    Three states of what he says

    Foam — the words

    Written along the newest wave in the order he speaks them. They last two waves and evaporate. A line the scene book cannot place leaves only foam and colour.

    Silt — colour and material

    Every word drops grains in its district's measured palette. Silt never leaves; it only dries and pales as later waves push it outward. Long words and slow delivery leave more.

    Stones — the places

    When a line names a scene, the wave carries that scene's archive views and sets them down. These are the buildings. The first line of the poem ends up as the outermost ring: the wall.

    Read the finished shore from the centre outward and it runs backwards through the poem, like a core sample. Read it as a plan and it is Plato's Atlantis: rings of land and water around a centre.
