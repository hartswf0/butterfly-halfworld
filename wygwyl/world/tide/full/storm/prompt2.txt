A film still, 16:9, photographed on 35mm, like a third-person video game. Seen from behind and a little above, in the lower middle of the frame, a young Black Ethiopian man in his twenties gallops a grey horse straight toward a colossal red sandstorm, a haboob wall of dust hundreds of metres high that fills the upper half of the frame and is only a few hundred metres ahead of him, its front boiling and curling, about to swallow the desert. He wears a white cotton shamma cloak with a woven red border flying behind him, a white head wrap, a leather round shield on his back and a long spear. Medieval Ethiopian highland desert around the twelfth century: red sand and black volcanic rock, a salt caravan of camels led by Black Ethiopian men in white cotton just ahead of him, already vanishing into the dust, a small rock-hewn stone church on a ridge at the edge of the storm. The light is dark orange under the storm, sun a pale disc. Epic and wide like a Sergio Leone western. No text, no letterbox bars.
