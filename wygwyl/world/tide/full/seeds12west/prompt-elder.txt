A film still, 16:9, photographed on 35mm. Third-person view from just behind an elderly Black Ethiopian woman in her seventies, seen from behind from the waist up, standing in the lower left-middle of the frame in the rough stone doorway of a dark Tigrayan stone farmhouse. She wears a white cotton netela shawl over her head and shoulders with an embroidered red and green tibeb border, and a long white cotton dress; one deep-brown hand rests on the stone doorframe. The doorway's stone edges and the room around her are near-black silhouette. Through the doorway: a vast red-earth highland plain at golden hour, flat-topped amba mesas and canyon walls on the far horizon, a lone sycamore tree on a ridge, and on a mesa to the right a tall slender silver fog-harvesting tower with mesh sails, a quiet piece of future technology. Very far away on the plain, tiny against the mesas, four men walk toward the house leading three donkeys loaded with yellow jerrycans of water, long shadows behind them, a thin trail of dust. They are so small they are only silhouettes. Composition like a Sergio Leone western: huge empty anamorphic landscape, the figure in the foreground, strong contrast, dust in the low sun. Grounded and humane like Haile Gerima's Ethiopian films: real cloth, real earth, dignity. Warm grade, fine grain. No text, no letterbox bars.
