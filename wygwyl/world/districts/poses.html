WYGWYL · world · where each view was taken

Each view placed where the city best explains it, in a language both can speak.

A photograph and a rendered frame are never compared as pictures here. Both are read into the same ten classes (sky, facade, roof, ground, water, planting, structure, rock, figure, vehicle): the photograph by panoptic segmentation, the city by its own semantic pass, every pixel naming what it shows and how far away. Every place a body can stand (each street every 30 m, rooftops, high points; eight headings, three pitches) is indexed in that language; each view keeps its best 40 under its own lens and horizon; an evolution strategy refines the 8 most different of them, scored on classes, depth (Depth Anything V2 on the photograph, the depth buffer in the city), boundaries, horizon, and a DINOv2 likeness between the two readings. A pose inside a wall or pressed against a surface is never accepted. Then two checks: does the frame found for a view pick that view out of all 100, and what do random poses score? The prompt that made each picture is read as a shot list: its lens is the lens, its vantage (platform level, aerial view, beneath an overpass) bounds the height and pitch, and pictures made from one prompt are searched again beside the best-placed one, since they are one place shot as different takes. Last, the eye says what each picture shows (BLIP's caption, CLIP's nearest words), so a result can be read without looking at it, and so the gap between the two becomes a list of what the city lacks.
