WHERE YOU GO WHEN YOU LEAVE · PART 2 OF 3: FUTURE Fourteen poems, fourteen halfworlds.

The beflix suite plays the film back: 128×96, eight shades, a recorded command stream and one mp3. This remakes each poem as its own world under the laws of I REMEMBER BEING A BUTTERFLY — eight ink levels and a single halftone pass, every movement a pure function of normalised time, every dissolve a per-dot allegiance swap rather than a cross-fade, and every sound synthesised at load.

Nothing here is a recording. There is no video file, no audio file, no sprite sheet and no build step. Each film is a few hundred lines of arithmetic that resolve to a picture eight times a second, which is why you can seek anywhere in one instantly and why frame 400 does not need frame 399 to have happened.

THE LAWS

The dot law. Everything is drawn in flat quantised tones into a 192×144 ink field and one ordered-halftone pass runs over the whole field at the end, so a body and a wall are quantised by the same lattice. There is no alpha, which means a dissolve cannot be a cross-fade — it has to be a per-dot allegiance swap on a Bayer schedule, and that reads as one substance replacing another rather than two briefly coexisting. Poem 01 is about a haze claiming a room and poem 14 is about everything learning to be weather. The constraint and the content agree.

Pure time. Every movement is draw(u, F) — a function of normalised time and nothing else. No wall clock, no accumulated state, no reading the previous frame. Motion blur is therefore not a trail that was stored; it is the same pure function sampled two more times, a little way into its own past, and drawn lighter.

Sound no smoother than the picture. The bed is three detuned partials whose root steps with the movements. Foley is a struck body: a few decaying partials for the material, a burst of shaped noise for the contact, and no tail longer than the room it rings into — a long reverb tail is the audio equivalent of a gradient, and this world does not have those.
