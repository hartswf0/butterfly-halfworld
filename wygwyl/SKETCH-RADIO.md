# SKETCH RADIO — where the two pages meet

Two pages, each holding the half the other is missing.

**SKETCHSONG** (`k2.html/word-sketch-song`) has the better **idea**. `FROM →
THROUGH → TO`: a word or a drawing becomes a piece of music, the translation
theory is written down where it can be argued with, the result is drawn back as
a spectrum, and marks you make on that spectrum feed the next version. Six
visible construction stages, a branchable history, and *stones* — translation
rules worth keeping. It treats a mapping as a claim rather than a fact.

**WYGWYL RADIO** has the better **channel**. A frame of ink transmitted as sound
comes back out of a Fourier transform correlating **0.918** with the frame that
went in, and fourteen prosodic engines decide how a picture is read *in time*.

## THE GAP, STATED EXACTLY

Sketchsong's loop never closes in signal. Its spectrogram is a picture *of* the
audio — a 512-point FFT of a 12 kHz offline render — and every arrow back out of
it runs through a language model: the marks are sent as a PNG to a vision model,
described in words, and a second model recomposes the notes. Nothing in that
chain puts energy at 400 Hz because you drew at 400 Hz. It is a semantic loop
wearing the clothes of a signal loop.

The giveaway is in its own schema. `PIPELINE_SCHEMA` requires sixteen synthesis
effects — `delay, reverb, resonance, cutoff, distortion, noise, knock, mallet,
ring_modulation, ring_mod_pitch, tremolo_rate, decay, tremolo, sustain, release,
attack` — every one of them generated by the model, stored in the pipeline JSON,
carried through history, and exported. Grepping the synthesiser for each:

```
delay 0 · reverb 0 · resonance 0 · distortion 0 · noise 0 · knock 0
mallet 0 · ring_modulation 0 · tremolo 0 · decay 0 · sustain 0
release 0 · attack 0 · cutoff 2
```

**One of sixteen is wired.** Valid JSON, real names, legal types, and fifteen
rules that do nothing — with no error message, ever. That is not a criticism of
the idea; it is the precise thing a signal-closed loop makes impossible, because
a parameter that does nothing shows up immediately as a picture that does not
change.

## WHAT THE MERGE ADDS

### 1. The spectrum becomes an instrument, not a report

What you draw *sounds* — now, with no key, no request, no round trip. The field
is the spectrum: rows are frequency, columns are time, ink is amplitude. There
is nothing to interpret because there is nothing in between.

### 2. The reading rate becomes a declared law

Sketchsong has bars and a tempo, which is a theory of **metre**. The fourteen
engines are a theory of **how a voice occupies time** — `sweep` is the breath,
`gate` puts silence at a place in the *line* rather than in the clock, `ground`
decides how much of the background may speak, `attack` is the difference between
a wash and a statement. The same drawing under *gabay* and under *imzad* is two
different pieces: one exact and unaccompanied, one enormous and mostly silence.

### 3. The theory becomes testable

Sketchsong's THEORY panel asserts a mapping — "density → note density." This
page measures it. The analyser's reading is written into the column the head is
currently sounding, so the return picture **accumulates into the same geometry
you drew in** rather than scrolling past, and the correlation between the two is
on screen while it plays. A mapping that cannot survive the round trip is a
decoration, and now the number says so.

### 4. `HEARD → FIELD`

Sketchsong has this button and it copies pixels of a picture *of* the sound into
the drawing. This one copies what was actually heard, quantised back to eight
levels, in the geometry it was drawn in. Press it repeatedly and you are
listening to a channel talk to itself — the Lucier operation, on a picture.

## WHAT IS NOT HERE

The language half, and it is not faked. There is no key gate and no network; the
seam where a word→sketch model would attach is marked in the page. Everything
here runs offline forever. `word ⇄ sketch` and the four-agent pipeline are
Sketchsong's, they work, and they would attach at the field — which is the point
of making the field the only document.

## WHAT THE NUMBER MEASURED

Offline, with a still frame, a 70-second transmission and a window sized to the
column, the round trip scores **0.918**. Live, in a browser, at a musical breath
rate, it does not — and the reasons are physics rather than sloppiness, which is
worth writing down because both were caught by the meter and not by reading.

**The analyser reports the past.** Its window is centred about ninety
milliseconds back, and at forty columns a second the head has moved four columns
on by the time the reading arrives. Written into the head's *current* column the
picture comes out sheared — and on a triangle scan it shears one way going out
and the other coming back, so the error doubles into a smear rather than an
offset. The scan is a known function of the clock, so where the head *was* is
computed rather than guessed.

**And the law I carried over from offline was wrong here.** Offline, the best
window is the one that matches the column — measured at 4096/8192/16384 the
fidelity ran 0.534/0.755/0.830 and the winner landed exactly on the column. So
the page was built to size its window from the breath. Fidelity fell from 0.604
to 0.277, and the sweep says why. At a breath of 7.8 s, whose column is 41 ms:

| window | | | window | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1024 · 23 ms | 0.287 | | 8192 · 186 ms | 0.718 |
| 2048 · 46 ms | 0.360 | | **16384 · 372 ms** | **0.753** |
| 4096 · 93 ms | 0.528 | | 32768 · 743 ms | 0.614 |

The optimum is **nine times the column** — and it is exactly what the rows
require: 144 of them across five octaves puts the tightest pair 7.35 Hz apart at
the bottom, and separating 7.35 Hz takes about 370 ms.

The offline law was not wrong, it was a coincidence. A 365 ms column was long
enough to satisfy both constraints at once, so the two answers agreed and only
one of them was the reason. At a musical breath they fight, and **resolution
wins**: unresolved rows are information that never arrives, whereas horizontal
smear is a known shift that has already been compensated for. Past the point
where smear overtakes resolution the curve turns over — that is the 0.614 at
743 ms, and it is where the cap comes from.

Sizing the window from the row spacing instead, the number stops depending on
the breath at all, which is the confirmation:

| engine | breath | fidelity |
|---|---|---|
| theydinn | 4.9 s | 0.711 |
| kabary | 7.8 s | 0.753 |
| imzad | 13.4 s | 0.736 |

## MUSIC AND ICON, AND WHY BOTH EXIST

The same field, the same law, the same analyser, two band banks:

| | bands | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MUSIC** | 56, quantised to a mode | **0.298** | in key, and the picture does not survive it |
| **ICON** | 144, continuous log | **0.744** | in tune with nothing, and the picture arrives |

That is not a defect to be fixed; it is the trade stated in numbers. Collapsing
144 rows onto seven pitches an octave is what makes any picture at all
listenable, and it is the same operation that throws the picture's vertical
resolution away. A page that only had MUSIC could claim the mapping worked. A
page that has both can show you what the claim costs.

## THE EARS-SUBSTITUTE

    node wygwyl/deep/listen.mjs gabay music 22
    node wygwyl/deep/listen.mjs kabary icon 20

Nobody building this can hear it, and "it seems to work" is not a claim. The
page publishes what it is doing on `window.__sketchradio`; `listen.mjs` drives a
real browser, plays a station, and reports the gate, the level, the window the
analyser settled on, and the fidelity. Every finding above came out of that loop
rather than out of reading, and two of them reversed a decision that had been
reasoned carefully and confidently in the wrong direction.
