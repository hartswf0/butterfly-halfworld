# OP-15 — the native field

    cut/out/op15/index.html

OPERATOR drew on **128×96**. Everything it was being asked to hold — the
halfworlds, MOUSSA, the beflix dot law, the 2,033 archive cut-outs — is drawn on
**192×144**. Both are 4:3, so nothing was distorted, but the ratio is exactly 1.5
and every import had to max-pool three cells into two.

**That is why a pick came back coarse.** Max-pooling merges whatever is adjacent,
so a window one cell from a wall arrives as the same cell as the wall, and the
parts you get back are blunter than the parts that went in. It is not a display
problem you can zoom past — the information is gone before it is drawn.

OP-15 runs at the native field. `hwDown` is a copy, not a pool. The `fit` control
that chose between max and mean is gone, because there is nothing left to choose
between.

## how anything gets into the frame

Both sources are on the **opening screen**, because "how do I load the worlds"
should not have an answer you need to be told.

    THE FOURTEEN · DRAWN     tap → the world is the frame
    THE ARCHIVE  · FILMED    tap → the piece arrives as a placement:
                                   drag it, ± to size, PLACE to commit

Nine faces of the archive sit there, one per noun — the pile's range rather than
its top. The other 2,024 are in **AUTHOR › Archive**, with the noun filter, and
the same tile can be dragged straight onto the board or pasted from the
clipboard.

**AUTHOR › Halfworld** is the full drawer: all twenty worlds, a time slider, the
part handles, `Stamp` / `Take them out`, `Stream the poemfield` and
`Ingest all fourteen`.

### verified in OP-15

    archive drawer          2,033 pieces · 16 nouns · sliders span 192×144
    SAM stamp               P006 the poet, 110 cells wide, face modelled
    world stamp             ATLANTIS from the empty frame
    both together           the poet with ATLANTIS's arches around him
    empty-frame archive tap P006_a-person_0 as a live placement

That last one is the point: a photograph and a drawn world in one frame, at one
resolution, neither of them resampled to meet the other.

## legacy strips still load

The `.twinstrip` format carries no width. But a film says so anyway: a 128×96 one
never names a column past 127, and a wider one reaches further within the first
few hundred frames. The extents are watched while it streams and the answer is
taken at the end — a measurement, not a guess. Scaling then happens in
`applyCmds`, the single place every frame is materialised, so **the stored text
is never rewritten** and the file that came in is the file that goes out.

    OP-14, 128×96 board, frame 1:  ink reaches 79% of the board
    OP-15, 192×144 board, frame 1: ink reaches 80%

Before the fix it was **53%** — exactly two thirds — the film sitting in the
left corner of a field it did not know had grown.

### the bug in the middle of it

`PNT` is `x y w h v` and `LIN` is `x1 y1 x2 y2 v`. My extent-watcher treated them
alike and added the far endpoint of every line to its near one, measuring a
maximum of **244** on a 128-wide film and quietly switching the scaling off.
Two commands, two grammars. Checked against the real 984-frame file rather than
in the browser:

    old grammar (add both): max x=244, y=172  →  not a 128×96 film
    new grammar           : max x=128, y= 95  →  a 128×96 film

## granularity is a separate axis

The native field makes a part **exact**. It does not make it **coarse**, because
a name is a class and not an object: `windows` means every window in the city,
which is the right answer to *what is this cell* and the wrong answer to *what is
this tower*.

So `WIDEN` is a second control. Each press adds everything touching what is
already held — one press takes a window to its tower, another takes the tower to
the row it stands in. Measured on ATLANTIS: `windows` → **+1468 cells** → the
whole waterfront. Coarse and fine are both a tap away, and neither is the tool
deciding for you.

## still on 128×96

OP-14 stays as it is. It is the tool the existing strips were authored in and the
one the OP-51 links open; OP-15 is for work whose source is butterfly-halfworld.
The two read each other's films — that is what the extent measurement is for.

Not ported: the Muybridge onboarding silhouettes are six packed 128×96 bitmasks
and are still unpacked at that size, so the gallop trace sits in the corner of
the larger field.
