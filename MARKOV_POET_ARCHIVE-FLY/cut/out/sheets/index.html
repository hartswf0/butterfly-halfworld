THE WOVEN SHEETS

The first city sheets pasted. Cut-outs sat on paper as discrete objects, however carefully placed, and nothing wove. The fix was to change what a MARK is.

beflix-E keeps the dot law — every cell of the field takes an ink level from the same local-contrast rule — and replaces the disc with an element from the archive whose measured ink weight matches that level. A dark cell takes a heavy element, a body or a wall; a light cell takes a thin one, a hand or a lamp. Each cell also measures the local gradient and picks an element whose aspect runs with the structure, so the tesserae lie along the image instead of across it. At a distance it is the picture. Up close it is four thousand people, windows and lights.

Weaving a finished sheet destroyed it — a composed sheet is mostly flat colour, flat colour has no local contrast, and the typography came back as mush. So the order inverts: weave the pictorial field first, print the graphics over it.

WHAT CHANGED FROM THE FIRST SHEETS

  • The ground is woven, out of the element library, at full sheet size. A plate sitting on it is a printed plate over a continuous field rather than one more rectangle in a stack.
  • Scale violence. One figure at 1.35× frame height, cropped by the edge, against lights at nine pixels. The earlier sheets kept everything between 0.22 and 0.38 of frame height, which is why nothing felt invented.
  • The tethers end on something. Each plate drops to the horizon and its foot is annotated with the noun the model used and how sure it was.
  • Three registers, because a continuum lost. A mosaic field of 9px marks, a middle tier of masses at 90–320px clipped to the field so each is cut to the shape it lands on, and a few plates at 400. A quadtree that let mark size vary continuously with the picture was built and beaten: big marks carry their own colour, dominate, and leave rectangular seams, and the portrait came apart into texture. Collage and mosaic are the same operation at different scales — but they want discrete registers, not a gradient.
  • The sheet states its own method. The band under the horizon carries the element census and the rows where the two describers disagree — what the hand-typed lexicon calls a shot beside what the panoptic model calls it. Herron's sheets are covered in text; here the text is true.
