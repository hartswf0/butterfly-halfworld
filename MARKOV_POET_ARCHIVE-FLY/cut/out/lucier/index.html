I AM SITTING IN A ROOM

I am recording the sound of my speaking voice and I am going to play it back into the room again and again until the resonant frequencies of the room reinforce themselves so that any semblance of my speech, with perhaps the exception of rhythm, is destroyed. — Alvin Lucier, 1969

Lucier's piece is not about degradation. It is about a message handed to a space until the space is all that is left of it. The words do not vanish; they are replaced, note by note, by the room that was carrying them.

That is the transition this work needed. Cutting from live action to code is a cut, and a cut asserts that two things are different. This asserts nothing: the photograph is played into the lattice, then that is played into the lattice, and again. There is no frame at which the image stops being a photograph and starts being code. Every generation is both. The seam is not hidden — it never existed, because nothing was joined.

THE ROOM, HERE

A 192×144 ink field, eight levels, and 2,033 cut-outs from the archive as its marks. Each pass re-describes the previous image in that vocabulary. Fine structure the lattice cannot hold falls out; structure that happens to lie along the lattice is reinforced — exactly as a room reinforces its own resonances.

It converges. What survives at generation eight of a shot of three people is three faint vertical traces — with perhaps the exception of rhythm. The positions where three bodies stood, and nothing of the bodies.

THE ARRIVAL FAILS, ON PURPOSE

There is no generation at which the message gets through. Ink falls to 0.044 and keeps falling. A human message sent somewhere it cannot quite arrive — which is the title of the suite, said in the one medium that can demonstrate it.
