INHABIT — the suite's own people, in a world we built for them

The fourteen halfworlds are not backgrounds. They are worlds with drawn people in them who already walk, stand, turn and fall. And the runtime knows exactly where those people are: renderScene(t, ids) returns the ink field, a parallel field saying which draw call owns each cell, and a cast in which every figure has deposited its guise, pose, facing, height and foot position.

So the character mask is not segmented. It is read. Nothing here is inferred from pixels. We are not guessing where the body is — we are the ones who put it there. In poem 06 that is 334 of 360 frames carrying figures: 2,511 everyman and 55 poet, walking and standing, across five named movements.

WHAT EACH FRAME DOES

  1. take the halfworld's ink field and its figure mask
  2. throw the background away
  3. put in its place a field built from the archive — sky, skyline, facade, stair, water — quantised by the same dot law, so it is the same material
  4. put the characters back on top, in their own ink, untouched
  5. light it, in the accent, after the ink — and a character is never the accent

The characters keep their animation because it was never touched. What changes is the world they walk through: a set assembled out of 2,033 photographed elements and then drawn, so that a figure computed by arithmetic and a window cut out of a film are, at the moment they meet, the same two inks on the same page.

TWO THINGS THAT HAD TO BE FOUND

The set has to carry weight or it is fog. Mapped linearly the field's pedestal left most inked cells at level one and the city read as haze behind the walkers. A gamma below one lifts the middle so a wall is a wall while the paper stays empty.

Where the people are. Three of the fourteen came back empty — their bodies do not appear in the first sixteen seconds, because a title or a landscape runs first. Each world is now scanned at 2fps and the densest window of population is what gets rendered. NEW DAY's people arrive at 24s, HOT MINUTE's at 70s.
