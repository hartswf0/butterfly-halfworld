DRESS — the archive fitted to the halfworld, and held there

No second segmentation pass was needed and none was run. A single frame of ADMISSION contains 803 distinct objects in the id field, already separated, because the runtime records which draw call owns each cell. Patching the kit to log the calling function gave every one of them the name its author wrote: windows, archWall, farShore, danceFloor, nave, brothers. Running SAM here would be asking a model to guess at things we wrote.

Most of it is not translated, and that is the point. petal, web, wavefronts, hourglass, templeGlyph, tambourine have no photographic counterpart, and inventing one would be dressing a computed thing in a costume. A dictionary that translates everything is not a dictionary, it is a fog.

THE FIT IS AN ASSIGNMENT, NOT A PICK

Hungarian per noun, across every slot at once, minimising total mismatch on shape rather than similarity of appearance: log-aspect weighted 1.7, bounding-box fill 1.0, tonal range 0.55. Both sides were measured months apart for unrelated reasons and turn out to be the same feature space. The mismatch number is diagnostic — windows fit at 0.592 with 248 candidates for 48 slots; the gate fits at 1.486 because only ten staircases exist in 135 shots and three slots share them. It says where the archive is thin.

AND THE BINDING HOLDS

A slot is keyed by its part name and coarse position, so the same hole keeps the same photographed window for as long as it exists — 156 slot bindings across a fourteen-second sequence. Re-drawing the lottery each frame is what made every earlier attempt read as noise.

BODIES ARE CAST BY WHAT THEY ARE DOING

The cast reports mode per body per frame. A standing figure is narrow, a walking one wider, a sitting one wider and shorter — and a falling one is drawn from "a person in mid-air", the phrase I nearly deleted for firing on every shot in the archive. In a suite about leaving that promiscuity was never noise: it is 57 photographed bodies not touching the ground, and THE FALL is where they belong.

These are the loose cut — each element keeps its own outline and is only positioned where the part was, so the archive gets to contradict the drawing. The fitted cut, which clips each photograph to the author's silhouette, is also on disk.
