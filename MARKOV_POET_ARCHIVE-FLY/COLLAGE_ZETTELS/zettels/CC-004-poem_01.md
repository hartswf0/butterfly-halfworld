ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/collage/poem_01.webp — poem 01, OUT OF LIFE — 1,106 elements from 67 shots.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Any-space-whatever is the genetic affection-image: an indeterminate space capable of expressing nonhuman qualities and powers.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The accumulated elements of one poem, laid over one another as a single field.

LOCAL MOVE:
Sixty-seven shots of one bedroom are laid over each other until the room stops being a room. Teal and slate; no edge holds; a window is implied by lightness rather than drawn. The most heavily shot poem produces the least legible place.

SOURCE TERMS:
any-space-whatever
composition
affect
field

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
More coverage made less location. Density here is a solvent.

QUESTION:
At what point does accumulation stop describing a room and start describing the fact of having been in one too long?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does a still made by accumulation already contain its own duration?

MECHANISM:
one poem's shots
→ segmented into elements
→ laid over one another
→ a single field carrying the poem's operation.

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

A dim teal room that never resolves. Hold one fixed frame for four minutes. Furniture edges assemble and dissolve without ever agreeing. A window brightens somewhere left of centre and is gone. Vapour moves; nothing else does. No one enters. The room must feel inhabited by having been looked at too many times, not by anyone being in it.

TENSION:
With 1,106 elements the field risks becoming mud rather than indeterminacy.

MISSING:
A measure of when accumulation stops adding and starts erasing.

BOUNDARY:
A layered still ≠ the sign it resembles.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
Remove half the elements at random. If the operation survives, the sign was
carried by structure rather than by density.
