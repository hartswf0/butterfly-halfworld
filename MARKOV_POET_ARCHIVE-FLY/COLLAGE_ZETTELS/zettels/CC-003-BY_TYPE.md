ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016 — Figure II.31, p. 133.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/collage/BY_TYPE.webp — sixteen category-composites, each
captioned with its instance count, shot count and ground.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
A mark is a relation discovered across a habitual or repeating series of images.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Every instance of one noun in the archive, laid over itself.

LOCAL MOVE:
No single "window" is the window. The word becomes visible only as the residue of
248 windows from 50 shots agreeing about where windows tend to be. Meaning is in
the recurrence, not in any instance.

SOURCE TERMS:
mark
relation
series
habit

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The stack is an average, and an average of a noun looks like a place.

QUESTION:
How many instances are needed before a category becomes an image?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is this a mark, or a picture of the segmenter's prior?

MECHANISM:
instances of one noun
→ overlaid
→ agreement darkens, disagreement fades
→ the noun becomes a shape.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<INSTANCES>
→ <SERIES>
→ [RELATE]
→ <MARK>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Mark is the first relation-image sign.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

Sixteen tiles, each a word becoming visible. Begin with all sixteen black. One at
a time, instances arrive and stack — 499 lights, 316 people, 248 windows, 10
doors — each arrival slightly brightening the tile and slightly shifting its
shape. Show the count rising in the corner. The film is over when every word has
been reached by enough examples to look like a thing. Where a word has too few
examples, leave it sparse and unresolved, and hold on it longest.

TENSION:
Repetition here is statistical, not habitual; the series is a population, not a
sequence a viewer lived through.

MISSING:
A distinction between mark and motif.

BOUNDARY:
Averaging ≠ mark unless it organises understanding.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[CINEOSIS-010-31]] → Deamer II.31 → Se7en → this sheet.

TEST:
Randomise which instances go in which tile. If the tiles still look like nouns,
the relation was not doing the work.
