ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/collage/type_a-neon-sign.webp — every "a neon sign" in the archive,
42 instances across 7 shots, laid over itself on night.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The lectosign is the limiting read-image: visible fragments demand virtual relinking through interpretation rather than completing themselves on-screen.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
One noun of the segmenter's vocabulary, as a picture.

LOCAL MOVE:
Vertical coloured strips on black, each of them once a word. The cutting kept the light and discarded the legibility. The image is made of things that mean and cannot be read.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Seven shots supplied forty-two signs. The densest category by shot is the one that says least.

QUESTION:
Why does a taxonomy of images end in a demand upon reading rather than in another sign?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is a category an image, or only the shadow of the instrument that named it?

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

Black. Vertical strips of saturated neon colour, each obviously a word, none of them resolvable. Move past them as if along a street. Occasionally two strips align and almost form a phrase before separating. Show partial letters. Show a sign reflected in water where it is briefly more readable than the sign itself. Never let a full word be read. The viewer must assemble the street's language and fail.

TENSION:
Illegibility can be a style; it becomes a lectosign only if the reading is genuinely demanded.

MISSING:
A way to see the segmenter's prior separately from the world it was looking at.

BOUNDARY:
An average ≠ the sign it resembles.
