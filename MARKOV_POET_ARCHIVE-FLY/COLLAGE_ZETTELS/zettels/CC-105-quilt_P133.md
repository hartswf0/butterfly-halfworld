PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The imprint is the indelible impression a determined world leaves upon character
and conduct.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Three people whose substance is the film they are in.

LOCAL MOVE:
The figures are not photographed and not drawn. Every square inch of them is
another moment of the same archive — a window here, a shoulder there, a strip of
neon in the jaw. The world has not merely shaped them; it is what they are made
of. And the ground between them is left empty, so the bodies are the only place
the world has accumulated.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Legibility survives at a distance and disappears up close. Step back and it is
three people; step in and there is no one there, only the film.

QUESTION:
How can cinema show environmental inscription without turning it into
psychological determinism?

DEEPER QUESTION:
If a body is made of its world, is it a character or a location?

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

Three people standing in an empty pale room. Seen from across the room they are
ordinary. As the camera approaches, their skin, clothes and hair are revealed to
be made of small photographic fragments of everywhere they have ever been —
windows, signs, other people's hands, a stair, a light. When they move, the
fragments do not move with them; the fragments stay where they are and the
bodies slide across them, taking new material. Nothing else is in the room. The
only place the world exists is inside them.

TENSION:
Mosaic portraiture is a technique with its own history, and it can supply the
effect without the inscription.

MISSING:
A way to distinguish durable disposition from surface decoration.

BOUNDARY:
A photomosaic ≠ an imprint.

TEST:

recognisably of this world, the imprint is real.
