ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016 — Figure II.43, p. 160.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/patchfield/modes.webp — the same two layers rendered through
every blend the field supports: over, behind, deepen, lift, darkest, lightest,
differ, mask, knock.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"force us to think"

[PARAPHRASE]
Cinema of the brain treats screen-space as cerebral organization: relations
become directly arranged in the visible field.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The set of possible relations between two images, shown as one image.

LOCAL MOVE:
Nothing is depicted. What is on screen is the operator table of the medium — the
complete list of ways one layer can meet another, held side by side so the
differences between them are the content.

SOURCE TERMS:

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A tool's settings panel turned out to be the most explicitly cinematic image in
the set, because it shows relation with nothing else in the way.

QUESTION:
When does a diagram become cerebral rather than merely instructional?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is a grammar a picture?

MECHANISM:
two layers
→ every available operation
→ laid out together
→ the grammar becomes visible.

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

Nine panels, all showing the same two images meeting in nine different ways:
one covers, one hides behind, one adds weight, one takes weight away, one keeps
the heavier, one keeps the lighter, one keeps the difference, one keeps only what
the other touches, one removes only what the other touches. Run all nine at once
from the same source footage. Do not label them. Let the viewer learn the grammar
by watching the same event nine ways, and end on the panel where the two images
have destroyed each other.

TENSION:
A settings chart is instructional by intent; its cinematic status is a claim
about how it is read.

MISSING:
A criterion distinguishing cerebral organization from documentation.

BOUNDARY:
A reference chart ≠ cinema of the brain.

TEST:
