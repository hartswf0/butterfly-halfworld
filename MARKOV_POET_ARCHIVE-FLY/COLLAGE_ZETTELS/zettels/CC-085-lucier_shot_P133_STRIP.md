ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016 — Figure II.31, p. 133.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/lucier/shot_P133_STRIP.webp — all nine generations of a frame of three figures from shot P133 in one row.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
A mark is a relation discovered across a habitual or repeating series of images.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The recursion laid out flat so the series can be seen as a series.

LOCAL MOVE:
No single generation states the operation. Laid side by side, the strip does: a
photograph enters at the left and leaves at the right as a scattering, and the
descent is monotone. The meaning is in the interval between adjacent frames.

SOURCE TERMS:
mark
relation
series
habit

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The strip is the only frame in the set that contains an argument, and it contains
no new material at all.

QUESTION:
How many repetitions are needed before a relation becomes a mark?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is generation loss a habit, in Deamer's sense, or only a slope?

MECHANISM:
nine generations
→ laid in order
→ the difference between neighbours becomes visible
→ the process becomes the subject.

OUR FORMALIZATION:

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

Nine panels in a row, all playing at once, left to right: a photograph of three
people, and eight successive re-descriptions of it, each thinner than the last.
Never cut. Let the viewer's eye do the travelling. As the film runs, each panel
loses a little more, in step, so the whole row descends together. The subject of
the film is the interval between neighbours, and the film is over when the
rightmost panel is empty and the leftmost is still a photograph.

TENSION:
A monotone decay is a slope, and a slope may not be a habit.

MISSING:
A distinction between mark and gradient.

BOUNDARY:
A progression ≠ a mark unless it organises understanding.

TEST:

doing the work.
