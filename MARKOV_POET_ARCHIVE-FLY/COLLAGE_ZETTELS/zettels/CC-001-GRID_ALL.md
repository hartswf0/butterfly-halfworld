ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016 — Figure II.03, p. 80.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/collage/GRID_ALL.webp — 2,033 segmented elements from 135 shots,
laid on one cream field by cut/collage.py.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Gaseous perception is genetic perception: images resonate with one another in an
acentred system rather than orbiting a privileged character.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The complete cut archive as a single flat field.

LOCAL MOVE:
Nothing is foreground. Scale is uniform, so a face and a lamp occupy the same
authority. What organises the image is not subject matter but DENSITY — horizontal
bands where the segmenter found many things and pale voids where it found few.

SOURCE TERMS:
gaseous perception
acentred system
molecular
resonance
genesis

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The bands are not composed. They are a histogram of the segmenter's attention,
and they read as landscape anyway.

QUESTION:
Is a field acentred if its structure is an artefact of the instrument that made it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does machine segmentation produce gaseous perception by default, since it has no
reason to privilege anything?

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

No protagonist and no horizon. Two thousand small photographic fragments hang in a
pale field and drift at different speeds — a hand, a window, a strip of neon, a
shoulder. Nothing enters and nothing exits. Bands of density slowly migrate
upward like weather. Let fragments rhyme by shape rather than meaning: an ear
answers a doorway, a lamp answers an eye. Never let the camera settle long enough
for any fragment to become the subject. The film ends when the field has
redistributed itself, not when anything has happened.

TENSION:
A script placed these elements, so "acentred" describes the image-system and not
an absence of authorship.

MISSING:
A way to tell the segmenter's bias apart from composition.

BOUNDARY:
Many small images ≠ gaseous perception.
