ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016 — Figure II.43, p. 160.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/sheets/09_09-yet-heard.webp — the YET, HEARD cutsheet: a skyline built
from cut elements, callouts to the highest-scoring faces with their confidences,
ELEMENTS BY NOUN, and WHERE THE TWO DESCRIBERS DISAGREE.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"force us to think"

[PARAPHRASE]
Cinema of the brain treats screen-space as cerebral organization: relations,
partitions and ideas become directly arranged in the visible field.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
a blue burst with two silhouettes inside it, drawn as an argument about itself.

LOCAL MOVE:
The image is simultaneously the place and the reasoning that produced the place.
Leader lines run from a skyline down to a baseline of scores. The nouns are
counted. The two describers are shown disagreeing, by name, with the rows where
they failed to agree printed underneath.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The picture's uncertainty is part of the picture. A confidence of 0.872 is
composed into the frame with the same care as the skyline.

QUESTION:
When does diagrammatic mise-en-scène become cerebral rather than merely
informational?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is a map with its error bars showing the closest thing an archive has to thought?

MECHANISM:

→ placed as a city

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

A city drawn as a working diagram on a pale ground. Skylines assembled from photographic fragments stand over a baseline of numbers. Leader lines connect five faces to their confidence scores and the scores tick as the camera moves. In the margin a running tally of nouns rises. When the two describers disagree, print the disagreement over the image and leave it there. The camera never enters the city; it reads it. Nothing is resolved and nothing is hidden.

TENSION:
Diagrammatic staging can support conventional exposition rather than forcing
thought.

MISSING:
A criterion distinguishing cerebral organization from stylish infographics.

BOUNDARY:
Labels on an image ≠ cinema of the brain.
