ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/collage/poem_02.webp — poem 02, FLASHING LIGHTS — 16 elements from 1 shot.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
In the figure of the sublime, actions surpass what the apparently enfeebled situation seems capable of containing.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The accumulated elements of one poem, laid over one another as a single field.

LOCAL MOVE:
The poorest source in the suite — a single shot, sixteen cut pieces — produces the most declarative image: a hard orange field, a white staircase, a small pale figure ascending. Scarcity forced a poster.

SOURCE TERMS:
figure of the sublime
composition
affect
field

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The least material made the strongest picture. Every other poem had more and said less.

QUESTION:
Does an image become sublime because the means are visibly insufficient for it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does a still made by accumulation already contain its own duration?

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

A burning orange stairwell, flat as a screenprint. One small white cut-out figure climbs. The stairwell is far too large for the figure and the colour far too loud for the event. Nothing else exists. As the figure climbs, the orange intensifies until it is the only fact in the frame. Keep the means visibly poor — hard edges, no gradient, no depth — so that scale does all the work.

TENSION:
Graphic boldness can read as design confidence rather than as disproportion.

MISSING:
A measure of when accumulation stops adding and starts erasing.

BOUNDARY:
A layered still ≠ the sign it resembles.

TEST:
Remove half the elements at random. If the operation survives, the sign was
carried by structure rather than by density.
