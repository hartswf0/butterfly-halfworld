PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The body of attitude is a dispersed individuation: identity is distributed
through bodily attitudes rather than secured by one coherent body.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A figure whose parts have been substituted with parts belonging to others.

LOCAL MOVE:
The swap is only possible because the segmenter had already named the parts. Once
a body is a set of named regions, identity becomes an assignment, and the
question of who this is becomes a question about how many assignments can change.

SOURCE TERMS:
body of attitude
individuation
dispersion
part
noosign

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Nothing had to be drawn to make a new person. Identity turned out to be a table
of correspondences.

QUESTION:
How much continuity is required for several bodies to remain one character?

DEEPER QUESTION:
If parts can be exchanged freely, is the character the parts or the arrangement?

MECHANISM:
named parts
→ reassignment across figures
→ each body carries others' attitudes
→ individuation without unity.

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

One person, filmed continuously, whose parts are replaced one at a time by the
corresponding parts of other people — a forearm, then a jaw, then a shoulder,
then a gait. Each substitution is seamless and none is remarked upon. The
performance does not change; only the material performing it. Keep going past
the point where none of the original body remains and see whether the character
is still there. Do not cut. Do not explain. Never name anybody.

TENSION:
Part-swapping can read as a visual-effects demonstration rather than as dispersed
individuation.

MISSING:
A criterion separating this from anthology casting.

BOUNDARY:
Several bodies playing one role ≠ body of attitude automatically.

TEST:

one becoming-character.
