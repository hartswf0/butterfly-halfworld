ZETTEL

SOURCE:
David Deamer — Deleuze's Cinema Books — 2016.
OUR IMAGE: cut/out/collage/poem_13.webp — poem 13, HOW TO WIN MY HEART — 33 elements from 3 shots.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The body of attitude is a dispersed individuation: identity is distributed through bodily attitudes rather than secured by one coherent body.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The accumulated elements of one poem, laid over one another as a single field.

LOCAL MOVE:
One man in dark glasses stands in blue haze, but his outline is assembled from three different takes and does not close. Shoulders arrive twice at slightly different angles.

SOURCE TERMS:
body of attitude
composition
affect
field

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The doubling is small enough to look like motion blur and large enough to be a second person.

QUESTION:
How much continuity is required for several bodies to remain one character?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does a still made by accumulation already contain its own duration?

MECHANISM:
one poem's shots
→ segmented into elements
→ laid over one another
→ a single field carrying the poem's operation.

OUR FORMALIZATION:

TEXT-TO-CINEMA PROMPT:

A man in dark glasses in blue smoke. Film him as three overlapping performances of the same moment, offset by fractions of a second and a few degrees of posture. Never resolve them into one body. When he turns, the three turns do not arrive together. Do not use ghosting effects — shoot three real bodies occupying one intention. No name, no dialogue.

TENSION:
Multiple exposure can read as a look rather than as dispersed individuation.

MISSING:
A measure of when accumulation stops adding and starts erasing.

BOUNDARY:
A layered still ≠ the sign it resembles.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[CINEOSIS-010-41]] → Deamer → poem 13.

TEST:
Remove half the elements at random. If the operation survives, the sign was
carried by structure rather than by density.
